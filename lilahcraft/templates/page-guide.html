The PC

Craft a PC and place it anywhere. Right-click it for its nine Sites: the Crate store, Sell to Crate, the Marketplace, Card Packs, the Mini Museum, Mailbox and Orders, Trade, the Guide and the Courier job board.

Buying and shipping

Order from the Crate store and pick how fast it ships. Rush costs a little more; slower is cheaper.

If you’re offline or your inventory is full, the order waits in your Locker.

Selling

Selling to Crate is instant. What you get depends on how much of that item is already in stock, so selling into a full shelf pays less.

There’s a small gap between buy and sell prices, and daily limits keep one player from flooding a market. The Market page shows live prices.

Hot, deals and news

These tags show on the market’s signs, holograms and display boards in game, and on the Market page.

HOT
The price is up 8–15% for a couple of days: a good time to sell. While it’s HOT, everyone has a daily sell limit on it.
DEAL
The price is down 8–15% for a couple of days: a good time to buy. Buying has a daily limit.
UP or DOWN
News Flash: a sudden jump or drop of 15–25% that fades over about a day. At most two a day, between 7 am and 9 pm Central time, and only while someone is online.
WANTED
The shelf is sold out and the market wants some.

Seasons move some items by 3–4% for a while. Prices also drift about 1–2% a day on their own.

In game: the Market News screen, the Hot & Deals sort in the store, and a “While you were away” catch-up when you log in.

Player shops

The Marketplace is for everything the market doesn’t carry. Place a Pallet, stock it and set your price. Shoppers find it by department on any PC, and you get paid while you’re offline. The server keeps a small commission.

Your Pallet, Vending Machine or Display Case is yours: only you or staff can break it, and a townmate with build rights can’t. Whatever is in it goes back to your Mailbox, never on the ground.

Trading

Swap items and money with another player in one go. You both put things on the table, you both press Ready, and it swaps at once. Nothing leaves either bag before that.

Ask. Type /trade <name>, crouch and right-click the player with an empty hand, or use the Trade site on a PC. They get a Trade? screen and have 30 seconds to say yes.

The table. Yours is on the left, theirs on the right. Click a stack in your bag to put it on the table (right-click adds one more); click it on the table to take it off. Money goes on with the − and + buttons; tap the middle to change the step, from $1 to $1,000. Armour and the off-hand can’t be offered.

Ready. When you both press it: 3, 2, 1, swapped. Any change on either side un-readies you both, and Ready then waits a moment so you can look again. Giving something away for nothing takes a second tap. What doesn’t fit your bag goes to your Mailbox.

Closing the window, logging off, dying or changing world just stops the trade. Nothing has moved, so nothing needs giving back. A traded Mini changes owner in the Museum straight away.

What can’t be traded

  • Keepers: they’re yours for good.
  • Rented looks: the Arcade’s hats and caps that run out after 90 days.
  • Tokens, trails, golf balls and extra homes. They aren’t items.
  • A game’s own items, a Courier parcel and Museum display copies.
  • A collector cap the other player already has.
  • A shulker box or bundle with any of these inside.

Arcade prizes trade item for item only. No money in a trade with a prize on either table: a prize for a Mini or a stack of diamonds is fine. Looks and golf balls go to other players through Give a present in the Arcade.

Mail and the Locker

Mailboxes come in eight colours. An order that can’t reach you (you’re offline or your inventory is full) waits in your Locker until you pick it up.

Your Mailbox catches the rest: whatever didn’t fit your bag after a trade or a prize, and everything from a broken Pallet, Vending Machine or Display Case. Collect it at any Mailbox or on the PC.

Deliveries

Take a job from the Courier board on your PC: short runs, medium runs, and one long haul a day. You get a parcel and a destination. Near it, a small house appears with a villager out front. Hand over the parcel to get paid.

Pay depends on distance and how you travelled. On foot pays the most, horses and boats a bit less, elytra a lot less. Lose the parcel and you owe what the run was worth.

Runs are above ground only: the board won’t give you one while you’re in the Nether.

Towns

Found a town, claim land and invite players. Claimed land is protected: outsiders can’t build or break there, or put up a TV. Each resident adds to the land the town can claim, and you can buy extra land when you outgrow it.

Towns pay upkeep every day based on how much land they hold, and mayors set a daily tax to cover it. A town that runs out of money falls apart. Keep the bank topped up.

Crafting and storage

Linked Workbench

A crafting table that reaches into every chest within 8 blocks. On Java, what’s in them shows up in your empty inventory slots while it’s open: click to take. With JEI, click + and it fills the grid from your chests.

On Bedrock it’s a plain crafting table; the Combo Chest is your way into nearby chests.

Recipe: a crafting table in the middle, chests in the corners, copper ingots above and at the sides and an ender pearl below.

Combo Chest

One block that browses every chest within 8 blocks. Pull what you need, or empty your inventory into it and it puts things away. Works the same on Java and Bedrock.

Recipe: a barrel in the middle, chests in the corners, an ender pearl above, copper ingots at the sides and a comparator below.

Chest sorting

Double-click outside an open chest and it sorts itself. Pick a different trigger with /sort settings. With CraftBridge Client, middle-click sorts too.

On Bedrock there’s no outside to click: pick sneak-punch in /sort settings and crouch-punch the chest, or use /sort.

Server recipes

Some things that are hard to get here have their own recipes. Everyone finds them in the recipe book. JEI shows them on Fabric only; on NeoForge it knows just the game’s own.

A custom item from these recipes (one with a name of its own) can’t be placed: as a block it would turn into the plain one.

One punch picks up a Linked Workbench or a Combo Chest where you’re allowed to build, and neither burns or blows up. Java players who accept the CraftBridge pack when they join see a small table on legs and a chest whose lid opens while someone uses it; without the pack they look like a crafting table and a barrel.

Golem chests

Copper golems, hopper filters and item sorters decide where things go by what a slot already holds. If the Linked Workbench, the Combo Chest or the CraftBridge Client’s storage panel took the last item out of such a slot, the sorting would quietly stop working.

A golem chest is a chest, double chest or barrel that CraftBridge never takes the last item of any slot from. Storage always leaves one of each item behind, and the golems keep sorting into it.

Craft the Golem Chest Marker, hold it in your main hand and right-click a chest, double chest or barrel to mark it; right-click again to unmark it. You get a chat line and a sound, and the chest doesn’t open. A double chest is marked on both halves.

While you hold the marker, marked containers within 16 blocks sparkle. Only you see it.

Copper Golem Brush

A Copper Golem takes things from a copper chest and puts them into whichever chest is nearest. The Copper Golem Brush lets you pick its chests instead, so one golem feeds the ore room and another the farm.

Hold the brush in your main hand and tap the golem. For the next 60 seconds, tap each chest, trapped chest or barrel it may use; the chest doesn’t open, and its number floats over it while you hold the brush. Tap a linked chest again to unlink it. Tap the golem again when you’re done.

The golem still takes from any copper chest, and now puts things only into its own chests. With none of them in reach or with room, it holds on to the item and its name tag says so. A golem without a name is called Rusty while it has chests; a name you gave it stays.

To clear its chests, shift-tap the golem twice within 2 seconds. Every chest the brush links also becomes a golem chest, so storage leaves one of each item in it. Unlinking keeps that mark; the marker takes it off.

Sound Muffler

A block that hushes the sounds made around it. Place it and its menu opens; right-click it any time to change things.

Pick what to hush: a whole group (farm animals, bees, pets, villagers, golems, monsters, other players, footsteps, fishing, minecarts, pistons, doors and gates, bells, chests and more) or any single sound. Each click goes Normal → Quieter → Silent; right-click steps back. Minecarts go Normal → Silent only, and Quieter on Bees turns down hives and stings but not the buzz in flight.

Heard nearby lists every sound made in its area in the last 10 minutes: stand by the noisy farm, open it, click the culprit. Find a sound: type part of a name, like chicken or piston. A single sound can override its group, so villagers can be Silent while their trading sound is set to “Always play”.

It hushes sounds made inside its box for everyone, wherever they stand. Anyone can look at its menu. Break it and the item remembers its settings.

Some sounds can’t be hushed. They come from each player’s own game: rain and thunder, music and jukeboxes, furnaces and campfires crackling, portals, lava and your own footsteps. Rolling minecarts and bees in flight come from your game too, but Silent switches them off (never a TNT minecart: its hiss is a warning); set back to Normal, they’re heard again once you walk away and come back. Rule of thumb: anything in Heard nearby can be hushed, plus minecarts and bees through Silent.

Firework shows

Firework shows are prizes from the Prize Counter’s Fireworks & Trails tab, paid in tokens; prices are on the Arcade page. A show is an item: buy it, then right-click to set it off. It’s used up when it plays. Every firework is harmless.

The small shows go off around you. The Mega show and up fire from Firework Launchers, which you buy on the same tab and keep for good. Place them a few blocks apart, in a row in front of where you’ll watch, with open sky above. Stand within 32 blocks, face them and use the show: right-click a launcher, the ground or the air.

Fewer launchers than the show wants: it plays from the ones there are. None in range: the show stays in your hand. Anyone’s launchers work, the nearest first. The whole server is told who started a show.

Walking away doesn’t cut a show short. It ends early only if nobody is near enough to keep the launchers loaded, you leave the server or the server stops, and then there’s no refund. Watch from within about 30 to 40 blocks: rockets farther off may not be drawn.

The Firework Remote

The Remote picks which launchers fire, from anywhere. It’s on the same tab, kept for good and never used up. Hold it and tap a launcher: it’s linked as #1. Tap the next for #2, up to ten; that’s the firing order. Tap a linked one again to take it off. Then a Mega show fires from #1–#3, a Super from #1–#4, an Ultra from #1–#5 and an Ultimate from all ten, wherever you stand.

A launcher only fires while its area is loaded (someone within about 160 blocks); one that isn’t is skipped and you’re told. Rockets are drawn only within about 30 to 40 blocks of whoever watches, so link the launchers near where you’ll watch. Anyone’s launchers can be linked.

While you hold it, your linked launchers within about 96 blocks show their numbers, to you only. Tap the air or a plain block (on Bedrock, the ground) for My Show: your #1 to #10 with direction, distance and coordinates, Clear all, and Get my Show Map, a map 512 blocks across with your show’s launchers as numbered flags and your other launchers as white crosses.

Holiday colours

On seven holidays a year every long show (Grand and up) takes the holiday’s colours and some of its own shapes, like creeper faces at Halloween and stars at Christmas. A show keeps the look of the day it starts. Party Time and the 3-second show stay as they are.

TVs

A TV is a flat rectangle of blocks, on a wall, a floor or a ceiling, that plays YouTube videos and Twitch streams with sound. Everyone nearby sees the same moment of the same video.

To see and hear TVs you need the Collins mod: Java Edition, Minecraft 26.3, Fabric. Without it, and on Bedrock, a TV is just the wall it’s on. Get the mod on the Downloads page.

Make one. Look at one corner block and type /collins pos1, look at the opposite corner and type /collins pos2. Then /collins create <name>, /collins seturl <name> <link> and /collins play <name>.

The rectangle has to be flat, one block thick and at least 2 by 2. The picture stretches to fill it, so 16 wide by 9 tall keeps a video in shape. A name is letters, digits, _ and -, up to 32 characters.

Your TV is yours. Only you and staff can change or remove it; try someone else’s and you’re told whose it is. You can have three, and you can’t put one where you couldn’t build.

YouTube and Twitch. Any other link is refused with “Only YouTube and Twitch links can be played here.” Staff can approve other addresses: once they’ve put one on a TV, you can play, pause and reuse it. Private, members-only and age-restricted videos won’t play, nor will a premiere that hasn’t started. A live stream shows LIVE instead of a timeline.

The TV Remote

The remote does everything the commands do, from menus. Craft one (it’s in your recipe book) or type /collins remote. It looks like a glowing blaze rod.

Right-click while looking at a TV for its menu: play, pause or stop, back or forward 30 seconds, loop, YouTube quality, Set URL, the playlist, and Remove screen (click twice). Right-click anywhere else for your TVs and New screen: left-click one corner with the remote, right-click the opposite corner, then type the name in chat.

Set URL and Add a video take your next chat message as the link, and nobody else sees it. Type cancel to back out. While you hold the remote it does nothing else: no breaking, placing or opening.

Playlists

Each TV has one playlist. /collins playlist <name> add <link> puts a video at the end, and when one ends the next starts by itself. next, prev, play <number>, remove <number> and clear do what they say. In the remote’s playlist menu, click a video to play it and shift-click to remove it, or switch on remove mode (the shears) to remove with a plain click.

/collins loop <name> on replays a single video, or starts the playlist over from the top when it ends.

On your side. The mod fetches each video straight from YouTube or Twitch and keeps a copy on your disk; /collins-cache clear empties it. Your own picture limit starts at 720p: /collinsc quality 1080 or auto raises it.

Staying safe

SafeLamp

A craftable lamp that keeps hostile mobs away. Good for a front porch.

Graves

When you die, your things wait in a grave where you fell. You have three hours to get them back.
